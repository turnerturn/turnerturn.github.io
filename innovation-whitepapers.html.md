<h2 id="“our”-sdlc-context">“OUR” SDLC Context:</h2>
<p>We will maintain some flexible disciplines to promote our ability to achieve measurable results. We aspire to do impactful work and want to ensure we can demonstrate our output to leadership. The following cycles of tasks will help us produce repeatable success.</p>
<h3 id="articulate-the-solution">1. Articulate The Solution</h3>
<p>Got a spark of an idea or a solution that’s just on the tip of your tongue? Maybe you can’t quite explain it to a colleague yet, but you’re confident you can whip up a proof of concept in no time.</p>
<p>This task is all about making sense of your thoughts. It’s about connecting the dots and clearly defining the solution you want to innovate.</p>
<p><strong>Acceptance Criteria:</strong><br>
You have derived a “vision” resource using our “vision” markdown starter template. This document allows you to clearly articulate your idea to the team. At our next checkup, you will have the opportunity to present this draft and discuss your ideas with your colleagues. Be prepared to receive constructive feedback and take notes on any suggestions you might want to incorporate into your plan.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>NOTE:</strong><br>
While you probably have tons of awesome ideas, let’s start small with the first few iterations of our cycle. Once we’ve got a good grasp on our routine, we can crank up the difficulty and tackle bigger challenges._</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="deriving-the-milestones">2. Deriving The Milestones</h3>
<p>You’ve got you idea articulated in the sanboxing phase, and now you’re ready to start innovating!</p>
<p><strong>But hold on a second,</strong> let’s make sure you don’t bite off more than you can chew. Your idea is fantastic, but it likely has several components that will take time to develop.</p>
<p>Let’s break down this goal and derive some milestones.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>NOTE:</strong><br>
The goal is to improve our ability to break daunting tasks into achievable successes. At the completion of each milestone, you’ll have the chance to show off your progress to the team!</p>
<p>These milestones are flexible and entirely up to you. We encourage you to take small, manageable steps towards your vision. Some steps might be simple (e.g., requesting read-only access to a database), while others might be more complex (e.g., developing a software component that does all the things).</p>
</blockquote>
<p><strong>Acceptance Criteria:</strong><br>
The acceptance criteria for this task is simple: derive one or more tasks to add to your TODO column on your Kanban board. All tasks must be related to a single ultimate solution. We aim to practice good discipline and avoid the chaotic tangents that often arise during innovation.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>For example, if you come up with a second idea while breaking your current sandboxing idea into milestones, stash that second thought into your personal notes for now and stay focused on one idea at a time.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="tackling-the-milestones">3. Tackling The Milestones</h3>
<p>This is the fun part. Let’s innovate!</p>
<p><strong>Acceptance Criteria:</strong> You are making progress if you are moving the TODO items to the “DONE” column. After you have completed all of your TODO items, you will be ready for “Presenting The Product”. At this moment of your SDLC, a new TODO task will be added to your board for “Presenting The Product”.</p>
<h3 id="presenting-the-product">4. Presenting The Product</h3>
<p>This is the desired goal for everyone. After the milestones have been completed for a solution, we now get to show off to our colleagues. We can use this moment to practice our public speaking, enhance our technical writing skills, and incorporate a bundle of other soft skills. The output of this task likely includes a presentation, some whitepapers, learning moments, and other resources you would like to accompany your completed project.</p>
<p>Take a look at some of our samples in kanban screenshots below for inspiration on this task.</p>
<p><strong>Acceptance Criteria:</strong><br>
You have prepared a presentation and any accompanying materials (e.g., whitepapers, documentation) to showcase your completed project. You will present your work to the team, highlighting key achievements, challenges faced, and lessons learned. Be ready to engage with your colleagues, answer questions, and receive feedback.</p>

<h2 id="kanban-details">Kanban Details</h2>
<p><strong>How do we implement kanban?</strong><br>
We are all skilled individuals with unique inspirations. To foster an environment for our committee to pursue their individual innovation desires, we will have one kanban board for each individual.  Each individual will be responsible for keeping their kanban updated. When we periodically touch base as a team, we can consult each other and optionally review the status of an individual kanban. This periodic checkup can encourage us to collaborate ideas, coordinate breakout sessions between 1 or more individuals for consultation, and etc…</p>
<p>We will make use of Microsoft Teams to derive the idea of our kanban implementations. This will be convenient as we can hit the ground running without spinning cycles on the plumbing.  We can quickly share these task lists in the Microsoft Tasks app, we can conveniently print the details to pdf, and we can make use of the ability to connect to Tasks from our personal devices and modify our innovation ideas from resources outside of the org’s network. (See templates and samples in screenshots below)</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>NOTE:</strong><br>
Be sure to adhere to our non-negotiable and team norms as they are stated in the document above.</p>
</blockquote>
<p><strong>How do we define our kanban board’s categories?</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>TODO:</strong> Pending tasks for your goal.</li>
<li><strong>Working:</strong> Your task is in progress.</li>
<li><strong>Waiting:</strong> Tasks which are waiting for completion of work from someone that is not your self.</li>
<li><strong>Done:</strong> Your task has been completed. It will remain in this column until your entire vision is complete. On your next iteration of “our context of sdlc”, we will clear the board and get you prepped for the next cycle. (I.e… clear the board and add one “sandboxing” task to your todo column… )</li>
</ol>

We will make strategic use of our Microsoft Tasks application.</p>
